# Datum Collective — Epoch 2 Handbook

**Effective date:** 1 September 2026  
**Status:** Active

---

## 1. Purpose

This handbook explains how Datum Collective operates in practice during Epoch 2.

The **Epoch 2 Agreement** is the governing document. This handbook does not replace it or create a parallel set of constitutional rules. Where this handbook and the agreement conflict, the agreement takes precedence.

The purpose of this document is to make Datum understandable to its members: what the collective is, how people work within it, how projects are run, how communication should happen, and what members should expect from one another.

Datum should have enough structure to make serious work possible, but not so much structure that maintaining the organisation becomes the work.

---

## 2. What Datum Is

Datum is a **collegiate engineering collective**.

It exists to give technically curious people a structure in which they can pursue engineering and research problems together that would be difficult, less interesting, or less educational to pursue individually.

Datum is not primarily a club, startup, consultancy, or branding exercise.

A project does not need to become a product or generate revenue to be worthwhile.

A successful Datum project may produce:

- a working system;
- a prototype;
- software;
- hardware;
- an experiment;
- research;
- a technical report;
- an open-source project; or
- meaningful engineering capability and knowledge.

The quality of Datum is therefore measured primarily by the quality of the work it produces.

---

## 3. Epochs

Datum is organised into **Epochs**.

An Epoch is a defined period of operation with its own membership, projects, and governance framework.

An Epoch is not simply an academic year. It represents a reference point from which Datum can evaluate how it operates and decide what should change.

Historical Epochs should remain historically accurate. They should not be silently rewritten to reflect later membership, projects, or beliefs.

**Epoch 1** established Datum and tested whether the collective was worth continuing.

**Epoch 2** is the current operating period and begins on 1 September 2026.

A future Epoch should establish its own governing framework rather than silently modifying the historical record of Epoch 2.

---

## 4. Operating Principles

### Build over branding

Datum exists to do engineering. The organisation's public identity should support the work rather than substitute for it.

### Technical work comes first

Meetings, governance, documentation, and communication exist to make projects better and easier to execute. They are not ends in themselves.

### Own your work

If you take responsibility for something, you are responsible for moving it forward and communicating when that becomes impossible.

### Question assumptions

No technical decision becomes correct merely because a senior person proposed it.

Claims should survive scrutiny, testing, and evidence.

### Document what matters

Important knowledge should not exist only in one person's memory.

Another member should be able to understand enough of the work to review or continue it where reasonably practical.

### Finish things

A finished failure can be more valuable than an unfinished idea.

Experiments should produce evidence, prototypes should be tested, and projects should reach meaningful stopping points.

### Learn across disciplines

Datum is intentionally interdisciplinary.

Members are encouraged to understand the systems around their own speciality rather than treating disciplinary boundaries as walls.

---

## 5. Membership

Membership is a responsibility, not merely access to a Slack workspace or GitHub organisation.

Members are expected to:

- contribute meaningful work appropriate to their role;
- communicate reliably;
- participate in projects; and
- uphold the standards of the collective.

New members are admitted according to the Epoch 2 Agreement.

A member may leave Datum at any time by notifying the Cofounders.

Membership may be removed under the conditions defined in the Epoch 2 Agreement.

Membership should be understood as participation in a technical collective, not as employment.

Datum does not create an entitlement to salary, equity, profit, stipend, or reimbursement merely through membership.

---

## 6. Roles and Authority

Datum uses technical roles to establish responsibility and scope, not corporate hierarchy.

The current Epoch 2 structure is:

- **Cofounders** — responsible for overall direction, administration, continuity, and governance.
- **Founding / Lead Engineers** — hold significant technical responsibility within defined areas and may lead projects or technical workstreams.
- **Engineers and Contributors** — responsible for the work they undertake and for contributing meaningfully to projects.

A person's role does not automatically grant authority over unrelated work.

Technical authority should remain with the people doing the technical work.

A project may have a Project Lead who coordinates the project and is accountable for its agreed objective.

Collective-level governance remains subject to the Epoch 2 Agreement.

---

## 7. How Projects Work

A significant Datum project should have:

1. a clearly defined objective;
2. identified people responsible for the work;
3. an appropriate technical record or repository;
4. a tangible output, experiment, or research result; and
5. enough documentation for the work to be reviewed or continued.

A useful default project lifecycle is:

    QUESTION
       ↓
    RESEARCH
       ↓
    DEFINE
       ↓
    DESIGN
       ↓
    BUILD
       ↓
    TEST
       ↓
    MEASURE
       ↓
    ITERATE
       ↓
    SHIP / CONCLUDE
       ↓
    DOCUMENT

Not every project needs every stage in this exact order.

The purpose of the lifecycle is to prevent projects from becoming undefined collections of ideas.

### Project Leads

A Project Lead coordinates the work, keeps the project moving, and makes sure its objective remains clear.

A Project Lead does not automatically gain authority over Datum outside the project.

### Project Records

Technical work should live in an appropriate durable record.

Depending on the project, this may include:

- source repositories;
- CAD files;
- experiment logs;
- research notes;
- design documents;
- test data; and
- reports.

Slack should not be the sole repository of important technical knowledge.

---

## 8. Engineering Practice

Datum expects technical claims to be proportionate to the evidence supporting them.

Members should distinguish between:

- what was observed;
- what was measured;
- what was inferred;
- what is assumed; and
- what remains unknown.

Where practical, members should test important assumptions rather than relying on intuition alone.

**Failure is acceptable. Hiding failure is not.**

A failed test that produces useful information is legitimate engineering progress.

Repeated avoidable mistakes, unsupported claims, or work that produces neither a result nor a useful lesson should be reconsidered.

Technical review is normal.

A member challenging a design, calculation, experiment, implementation, or conclusion is not inherently challenging the person who produced it.

---

## 9. Documentation

Documentation exists to preserve useful knowledge, not to create paperwork.

At minimum, significant work should leave behind enough information to answer:

- What were we trying to do?
- Why did we choose this approach?
- What did we actually do?
- What happened?
- What did we learn?
- What should happen next?

Permanent knowledge should be kept outside ephemeral chat wherever practical.

A useful rule is:

> If someone will need the information again, document it somewhere durable.

---

## 10. Communication

Communication should be direct, technically useful, and proportional to the problem.

### Slack

Slack is primarily for:

- coordination;
- discussion;
- announcements; and
- quick collaboration.

It should not become the permanent source of truth for:

- project specifications;
- technical decisions;
- research results; or
- important records.

### GitHub

GitHub is the primary technical record for software and appropriate project artefacts.

Issues, pull requests, commits, repositories, and project documentation should be used where they provide a durable technical record.

### Documents

Long-form decisions, research notes, specifications, meeting records, and other information that needs to remain readable should be stored as documents or project records rather than buried in chat.

### Decisions

Important decisions should be recorded with enough context that another member can understand what was decided and why.

---

## 11. Intellectual Property and Attribution

Datum defaults toward open technical work where practical, subject to project-specific restrictions, third-party rights, and applicable licences.

Pre-existing work remains the property of its original creator.

Third-party and open-source work remains subject to its applicable licence.

Contributors to Datum work should receive appropriate attribution.

Members should not present another person's work as their own.

Formal ownership, licensing, commercialisation, or other legal arrangements should be addressed separately if a project requires them.

---

## 12. Public Representation

Datum is not confidential by default.

Members may generally discuss and publish their own work unless a project has an agreed restriction or another party's rights require otherwise.

Members should distinguish between:

- personal opinions;
- project-level statements; and
- official statements made on behalf of Datum.

Members must not make significant formal commitments on behalf of Datum without the required approval under the Epoch 2 Agreement.

---

## 13. Meetings

Meetings should exist because a problem benefits from synchronous discussion.

A meeting should ideally have:

- a clear purpose;
- the people who actually need to be present;
- decisions or actions as an output; and
- a lightweight record when the outcome matters.

Meetings should not replace technical work that could be completed asynchronously.

---

## 14. Conflict and Disagreement

Disagreement is expected in an engineering organisation.

Members should first attempt to resolve technical disagreements through:

- evidence;
- experiments;
- calculations;
- prototypes;
- documentation; or
- other appropriate means.

When a disagreement is not purely technical, members should address the underlying issue directly and professionally rather than allowing it to become a prolonged public argument.

The **Code of Conduct** defines the behavioural standard expected during disagreements and other interactions.

Where a matter affects Datum as a whole and cannot be resolved informally, the governance procedures in the Epoch 2 Agreement apply.

---

## 15. Joining Datum

A new member should be able to understand Datum without relying entirely on oral history.

A practical onboarding sequence is:

    JOIN
     ↓
    READ THE HANDBOOK
     ↓
    READ THE CODE OF CONDUCT
     ↓
    UNDERSTAND YOUR ROLE
     ↓
    JOIN RELEVANT COMMUNICATION CHANNELS
     ↓
    ACCESS RELEVANT PROJECTS
     ↓
    MAKE A FIRST CONTRIBUTION

The first contribution should be tangible.

The goal is not to test whether someone can memorise the handbook. It is to establish that they can participate constructively in the work.

---

## 16. Leaving Datum

A member may leave Datum by notifying the Cofounders.

When leaving, members should, where reasonably practical:

- hand over active responsibilities;
- document unfinished work;
- return or transfer collective resources where applicable; and
- make sure project collaborators know what remains outstanding.

Leaving Datum does not erase a person's contribution or authorship.

---

## 17. What Datum Should Avoid

Datum should actively resist becoming:

- an organisation that spends more time discussing projects than building them;
- a hierarchy where titles substitute for technical competence;
- a collection of abandoned ideas with no measurable output;
- a social server where project communication disappears into noise;
- a branding exercise with little underlying technical work; or
- a bureaucracy whose processes become more important than its purpose.

The simplest test remains:

> **Is this helping Datum do better engineering?**

If the answer is no, the process should be questioned.

---

## 18. Relationship to the Governance Agreement

This handbook should be read together with the **Epoch 2 Agreement** and **Code of Conduct**.

The hierarchy is:

    EPOCH 2 AGREEMENT
          │
          ├── defines governance and authority
          │
          ├── HANDBOOK
          │     └── explains operation
          │
          └── CODE OF CONDUCT
                └── defines behavioural expectations

The agreement governs where a formal rule is required.

The handbook explains how the collective should operate in ordinary practice.

The Code of Conduct establishes the behavioural baseline for members.
