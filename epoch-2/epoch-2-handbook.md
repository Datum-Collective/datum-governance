# Datum Collective — Epoch II Handbook

**Effective:** 1 September 2026  
**Status:** Active

---

## 1. What Datum Is

Datum is an independent engineering collective made up of technically capable students who happen to attend the same university.

It exists to bring together people with different skills, disciplines, experience, and ways of thinking so that they can do work that would be more difficult to accomplish individually.

Individually, members may pursue their own technical work.

Collectively, members can form teams around larger problems, contribute expertise to one another's work, and produce projects under the Datum name.

The long-term ambition is for Datum to become a place known for serious technical capability:

> If there is a difficult technical problem, Datum should be a place capable of figuring out how to solve it.

That capability is not assumed. It must be demonstrated through the work Datum produces.

---

## 2. Why Datum Exists

Datum exists for two closely related reasons.

### 2.1 To build

Datum provides its members with a group of people capable of helping one another build difficult things.

A member should not have to pursue technically ambitious work alone simply because they lack one particular skill.

Someone working on robotics may need software.

Someone working on software may need electronics.

Someone doing research may need mechanical design.

Someone designing a mechanical system may need simulation or control.

Datum exists so those capabilities can meet.

### 2.2 To become capable

Datum is also an environment in which its members can become substantially better engineers.

Members should gain experience that resembles real engineering work rather than merely participating in a conventional collegiate club.

This means dealing with:

- ambiguous problems;
- incomplete information;
- technical trade-offs;
- interdisciplinary collaboration;
- failed experiments;
- documentation;
- deadlines;
- testing;
- review;
- research;
- and actually finishing things.

The objective is not to make Datum look like a professional organisation.

The objective is to make the work produced by Datum comparable in seriousness to the work expected from one.

---

## 3. Epochs

Datum is organised into Epochs.

An Epoch represents a significant stage in the development of Datum rather than simply a calendar year.

Each Epoch provides a defined reference point from which Datum can evaluate its:

- people;
- projects;
- methods;
- governance;
- capabilities;
- and direction.

The name comes from the engineering and scientific use of a datum as a reference point.

An Epoch is therefore not primarily a milestone to celebrate.

It is a point from which Datum can measure where it is and decide where it should go next.

### 3.1 Epoch Transition

At the end of an Epoch, Datum should:

1. review the work completed;
2. review unfinished and abandoned work;
3. evaluate the effectiveness of its operating model;
4. review membership and responsibilities;
5. record significant lessons;
6. determine what should change;
7. establish the next Epoch;
8. draft the next governing documents; and
9. establish the next set of projects and objectives.

Historical Epochs should remain historically accurate.

A later Epoch should not silently rewrite the record of an earlier one.

---

## 4. Epoch II

Epoch II begins on **1 September 2026**.

Epoch II is intentionally small.

The initial membership consists of five people working across different technical disciplines.

The primary objectives of Epoch II are:

1. build serious technical projects;
2. develop the members through real engineering work;
3. establish a body of work that demonstrates Datum's capability;
4. develop a culture of rigorous technical collaboration;
5. establish Datum's credibility; and
6. determine what Datum should become in its next stage.

Epoch II is not primarily an expansion phase.

Membership is intentionally limited during this Epoch.

Questions surrounding broader recruitment, external membership, larger organisational structures, commercial activity, and expansion beyond the current university should be evaluated during the transition to a later Epoch rather than prematurely imposed on Epoch II.

---

## 5. The Standard

Datum should be judged by what it produces.

Not by:

- how many people it has;
- how many meetings it holds;
- how polished its branding is;
- how many social media posts it publishes;
- how many titles exist;
- or how sophisticated its organisational structure appears.

The relevant question is:

> **What did Datum actually build, discover, document, or ship?**

A Datum that looks impressive but produces nothing has failed at its primary purpose.

An organisation with five people that produces exceptional work has not.

---

## 6. Build Over Appearance

Datum should never become an organisation that spends more time appearing productive than being productive.

Branding, websites, posters, certificates, social media, governance documents, and organisational systems are useful only insofar as they support the actual work.

The existence of these things is not itself evidence of progress.

A project repository containing working software is progress.

A tested prototype is progress.

A research result is progress.

A useful technical document is progress.

A poster announcing a project that does not exist is not.

---

## 7. Technical Culture

Datum expects a high standard of technical competence while recognising that competence is developed rather than magically possessed.

Two principles apply simultaneously:

> **It is acceptable not to know.**

and

> **It is not acceptable to pretend to know.**

A member should be able to say:

> "I don't understand this yet."

The expected response is to learn it.

Members are encouraged to ask questions, study unfamiliar fields, seek help from other members, and develop competence beyond their existing speciality.

At the same time, technical claims should be supported by an appropriate level of understanding and evidence.

---

## 8. First Principles

Datum values first-principles thinking.

This does not mean that members must reinvent every tool, library, algorithm, mathematical result, or engineering technique they use.

It means members should understand the foundations and assumptions underlying the systems they depend on.

Members should be able to distinguish between:

- understanding a system;
- using a system;
- assuming a system works;
- and blindly relying on a system.

Existing tools should be used when they are appropriate.

The important requirement is that their use should not replace understanding.

Members are also encouraged to challenge conventional approaches where there is reason to believe a better solution exists.

---

## 9. Evidence Over Hierarchy

Technical authority does not come from seniority alone.

A founder can be wrong.

A lead engineer can be wrong.

A new member can be right.

If evidence contradicts an established opinion, the evidence should be investigated rather than dismissed because of who produced it.

Technical disagreement should therefore be normal.

A member should be able to say:

> "I think this is wrong, and here is why."

The standard is not whether disagreement occurs.

The standard is whether the disagreement is handled through reasoning, evidence, experimentation, and technical honesty.

---

## 10. Projects

Datum projects may originate in two ways.

### 10.1 Member-originated work

A member may begin a project independently.

If other Datum members contribute meaningfully, or the work becomes connected to Datum's collective purpose and resources, it may become a Datum project.

### 10.2 Collective projects

Datum may also form a team around a problem that the members collectively want to solve.

These projects are Datum projects from their beginning.

The distinction between individual and collective origin should not prevent collaboration.

Datum exists partly so that members can help one another with work that would otherwise remain individual.

---

## 11. What Makes a Datum Project

A project should generally become a Datum project when there is a meaningful connection between the work and the collective.

This may include:

- multiple Datum members collaborating;
- Datum resources being used;
- the project being undertaken explicitly under Datum;
- the project being proposed as a Datum initiative; or
- the project being materially advanced through Datum's collective capability.

A Datum project should have:

- a defined objective;
- people responsible for the work;
- a technical record;
- a meaningful output or research objective; and
- enough documentation for another member to understand the work.

Not every idea needs to become a formal project.

Ideas can remain ideas until there is enough commitment to act on them.

---

## 12. Project Scale

Datum does not optimise for maximum project size.

During Epoch II, the preferred model is a small number of serious projects that can be completed within a realistic period.

A project may take:

- several weeks;
- several months; or
- close to a year.

The important question is whether the scope is appropriate to the people, time, resources, and capability available.

Large projects are not automatically better projects.

A technically deep project completed well is more valuable than a grand project that never reaches a meaningful result.

---

## 13. Project Leads

A project may have a Project Lead.

The Project Lead is responsible for:

- maintaining clarity of the objective;
- coordinating contributors;
- identifying blockers;
- keeping the project moving;
- making sure important decisions are recorded; and
- ensuring that the project reaches a meaningful conclusion.

Project leadership is responsibility, not ownership of the people involved.

A Project Lead does not automatically gain authority over Datum outside the project.

Technical decisions should remain with the people who possess the relevant expertise, subject to the project's overall requirements and governance.

---

## 14. Research and Building

Datum does not treat research and building as separate cultures.

Research should inform engineering.

Engineering should generate questions for research.

A Datum project may therefore contain:

- literature review;
- mathematical modelling;
- simulation;
- experimentation;
- hardware development;
- software development;
- testing;
- analysis;
- or publication.

The appropriate balance depends on the project.

The standard is whether the work produces useful knowledge or a meaningful technical result.

---

## 15. Failure

Failure is an expected part of serious engineering.

A failed experiment is not automatically a failed project.

A failed experiment that establishes useful information can be a successful result.

A prototype that exposes a fundamental design flaw can be valuable.

A research hypothesis that is disproven can be valuable.

The important distinction is between:

> **failure that teaches something**

and

> **work that simply goes nowhere.**

Datum should record useful failures rather than hide them.

Repeated avoidable failure caused by poor communication, lack of preparation, refusal to test assumptions, or failure to act on known problems should be treated differently.

---

## 16. Shipping

Datum considers a project shipped when it produces a meaningful external artefact or result.

Depending on the project, this may be:

- an open-source repository;
- a working prototype;
- a hardware system;
- a public demonstration;
- a deployed software system;
- a research paper;
- a technical report;
- an experiment and its results;
- a dataset;
- a design;
- or another meaningful technical output.

Shipping does not necessarily mean commercial release.

It means the work has progressed beyond being an internal idea and now exists as something that can be examined, used, reproduced, tested, or built upon.

A serious Datum project should have a defined notion of what "shipped" means before the work is considered complete.

---

## 17. Documentation

Documentation is part of engineering work.

Important knowledge should not exist only in:

- someone's memory;
- private messages;
- temporary files; or
- Slack conversations.

Depending on the project, documentation may include:

- README files;
- design documents;
- CAD files;
- experiment logs;
- research notes;
- test results;
- specifications;
- architecture documents;
- issue discussions;
- or technical reports.

Documentation should be proportional to the project.

The goal is not paperwork.

The goal is that another competent person can understand enough of the work to inspect, reproduce, maintain, or continue it.

---

## 18. Communication

Datum currently uses multiple communication systems for different purposes.

### Slack

Slack is for:

- coordination;
- discussion;
- quick questions;
- announcements;
- collaboration;
- and social interaction between members.

Slack is not the permanent source of truth for important technical information.

### GitHub

GitHub is the primary durable technical record for software and appropriate project work.

Code, issues, pull requests, documentation, and project history should be kept there where appropriate.

### Other project records

Projects may use additional tools when required.

The principle is simple:

> **Use chat to communicate. Use durable records to preserve knowledge.**

---

## 19. Open Work

Datum's technical work should be open by default.

Project work produced under Datum should generally be made openly available through appropriate public repositories, publications, documentation, or other means.

The purpose is to:

- allow others to learn from the work;
- allow external contribution;
- make the work reproducible;
- establish a public record of Datum's output; and
- contribute to the broader technical community.

Exceptions may exist where necessary because of:

- third-party intellectual property;
- licensing restrictions;
- privacy;
- safety;
- security;
- or another legitimate restriction.

Such exceptions should be deliberate rather than the default.

---

## 20. Attribution

Open work does not mean anonymous work.

People who materially contribute to a Datum project should receive appropriate attribution.

Contributors should not be removed from project records merely because the project is published under the Datum name.

A Datum project should make it reasonably clear:

- who contributed;
- what they contributed where useful; and
- what the project is.

Datum represents collective work, not the erasure of individual contribution.

---

## 21. External Contributions

Datum should be open to useful external contributions where appropriate.

External contribution may eventually include:

- code contributions;
- technical review;
- research collaboration;
- design feedback;
- documentation;
- testing;
- or other expertise.

During Epoch II, external participation is not yet a primary organisational objective.

The infrastructure should nevertheless be designed so that future collaboration is possible.

---

## 22. External Requests and Problems

One long-term ambition of Datum is to become a place where difficult technical problems can be brought to the collective.

During Epoch II, however, Datum is primarily focused on building its own capability and body of work.

External requests should therefore be evaluated based on:

- technical value;
- member interest;
- available capacity;
- alignment with Datum's purpose;
- and whether taking the work on would compromise existing projects.

Datum should not accept work simply because it has been asked.

The collective's credibility is more important than appearing busy.

Any formal external commitment remains subject to the Epoch 2 Agreement.

---

## 23. Money and Resources

During Epoch II, Datum's financial purpose is to support the work.

Collective funds or external funding may be used for legitimate Datum purposes such as:

- equipment;
- software;
- materials;
- fabrication;
- infrastructure;
- hosting;
- research resources;
- or necessary travel.

Membership itself does not create an entitlement to payment.

Money should not become the primary reason a project exists.

Commercial activity, revenue generation, sponsorship structures, and other significant financial models should be considered as part of a future Epoch rather than assumed during Epoch II.

---

## 24. Membership

Datum is currently limited to students from the same university.

Membership is not intended to be a popularity contest or a conventional club recruitment process.

A person may be invited to Datum when existing members see strong evidence of the qualities required to contribute to the collective.

These qualities may include:

- technical ability;
- curiosity;
- persistence;
- willingness to learn;
- intellectual honesty;
- reliability;
- ability to work with others;
- and evidence of actually building or learning things.

Existing expertise is not the only criterion.

Someone who is currently inexperienced in a particular technical field may still be valuable if they demonstrate the ability and commitment to become capable.

The final admission authority is defined by the Epoch 2 Agreement.

---

## 25. Participation

Datum does not require members to contribute identical amounts of time.

Different members will have:

- different projects;
- different responsibilities;
- different academic commitments;
- and different areas of expertise.

Contribution should therefore be judged by responsibility and meaningful participation rather than by a simplistic number of hours.

A member who takes responsibility for a substantial technical component is contributing even if their work looks very different from another member's.

At the same time, accepting responsibility and then repeatedly failing to communicate or contribute is incompatible with a functioning collective.

---

## 26. Leaving

Members may leave Datum.

Leaving does not erase previous contributions or authorship.

Where practical, members leaving an active project should:

- document their current work;
- communicate outstanding tasks;
- transfer relevant files or access;
- and leave the project in a state that others can continue.

The purpose is continuity, not punishment.

---

## 27. Roles

Datum uses titles to communicate responsibility.

Titles should not be treated as indicators of personal worth, intelligence, or superiority.

A Cofounder may spend most of their time doing robotics.

A Mechanical Engineer may contribute to software.

A person with a senior title may learn from a person with a junior title.

The useful question is:

> **Who is best positioned to handle this problem?**

not:

> **Who has the highest title?**

---

## 28. Intellectual Honesty

Members are expected to represent their work accurately.

Do not:

- claim an experiment succeeded when it did not;
- claim understanding you do not possess;
- exaggerate results;
- hide significant failures;
- misrepresent another person's contribution;
- or present assumptions as established facts.

When uncertain, say so.

When wrong, correct it.

When evidence changes the conclusion, change the conclusion.

This is fundamental to the technical credibility of Datum.

---

## 29. Technical Review

Members should expect their work to be questioned.

Review may involve:

- asking why a design was chosen;
- challenging assumptions;
- checking calculations;
- reproducing experiments;
- testing code;
- reviewing architecture;
- examining evidence;
- or proposing alternatives.

Review is not an attack on the person who produced the work.

The purpose is to make the work better.

Members should apply the same standard to their own work that they apply to others.

---

## 30. Culture

Datum should cultivate:

- curiosity;
- persistence;
- intellectual honesty;
- technical competence;
- interdisciplinary thinking;
- independence;
- craftsmanship;
- evidence-based reasoning;
- and willingness to help others.

Datum should actively resist:

- ego-driven hierarchy;
- résumé padding without meaningful contribution;
- performative productivity;
- unnecessary bureaucracy;
- abandoned projects presented as achievements;
- technical gatekeeping for its own sake;
- and organisational activity that exists mainly to look impressive.

---

## 31. The Datum Test

When deciding whether a process, project, meeting, document, role, or activity is worthwhile, ask:

> **Does this make Datum better at doing serious technical work?**

If yes, it is probably worth doing.

If not, its necessity should be questioned.

This applies equally to engineering and administration.

---

## 32. What Success Looks Like

At the end of an Epoch, Datum should be able to point to things that did not exist before the Epoch began.

That may include:

- working systems;
- research;
- software;
- hardware;
- publications;
- experiments;
- technical knowledge;
- open-source projects;
- or other meaningful engineering outputs.

The number of projects is less important than their substance.

A small number of exceptional results is preferable to a large catalogue of superficial activity.

---

## 33. The Standard for Epoch II

Epoch II should be considered successful if it establishes credible evidence that Datum's model works.

That evidence should come primarily from the work.

By the end of the Epoch, someone unfamiliar with Datum should be able to examine its public output and conclude:

> **These people are serious about engineering.**

That is the standard.

Not the number of members.

Not the number of posts.

Not the number of certificates.

Not the appearance of the organisation.

The work.

---

## 34. Relationship to Governance

This handbook explains how Datum operates.

The **Epoch 2 Agreement** defines the governing rules and authority of the collective.

The **Code of Conduct** defines the minimum behavioural standard expected of members.

Where the documents conflict:

1. The Epoch 2 Agreement governs matters of authority and formal governance.
2. The Code of Conduct governs behavioural standards.
3. This Handbook explains ordinary operating practice.

The governance repository is the durable historical record of these documents and their changes.

## 35. Status

**Datum Collective — Epoch II**

**Effective:** 1 September 2026

This handbook may be revised during Epoch II when experience demonstrates that a change is necessary.

Revisions should be deliberate, documented, and committed to version control.

The purpose of the handbook is not to predict every situation Datum may encounter.

It is to provide a useful operating model while leaving enough freedom for the collective to actually build.
