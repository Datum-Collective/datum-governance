# Datum Collective — Epoch 2 Agreement

**Effective:** 1 September 2026  
**Status:** Active

---

## 1. Purpose

This Agreement establishes the governance of Datum Collective during Epoch 2.

Datum is an independent engineering collective whose members currently happen to attend the same university.

Datum is not a university club and is not governed by university club structures unless explicitly agreed otherwise.

Datum exists to bring together technically capable people with different skills, experience, and areas of expertise so that they can pursue difficult engineering and research work together.

Members may pursue individual technical work, collaborate on one another's work, or form collective teams around larger projects.

The purpose of Epoch 2 is to develop Datum's capability and establish its credibility through serious technical work.

---

## 2. Epoch 2 Objectives

Epoch 2 is intentionally small.

Its objectives are to:

- build technically ambitious projects;
- develop members through real engineering work;
- combine expertise across disciplines;
- conduct meaningful research;
- document technical work;
- produce open technical work;
- ship tangible results; and
- establish a credible body of work for Datum.

Epoch 2 is not primarily an expansion phase.

Questions concerning broader recruitment, external membership, commercial structures, significant funding, or expansion beyond the current university should be evaluated as part of a future Epoch.

If Epoch 2 concludes without meaningful work having been shipped, that should be considered a major failure of the Epoch.

## 4. Leaving and Removal

A member may leave Datum at any time by notifying the Cofounders.

Leaving Datum does not erase a person's previous contribution or authorship.

Where practical, a departing member should document and transfer relevant project information so that active work can continue.

A member may be removed for:

- sustained non-participation;
- serious misconduct;
- deliberate dishonesty;
- misuse of Datum resources;
- or conduct that materially harms the collective.

Removal requires unanimous approval of the remaining Cofounders.

Removal ends the person's authority to represent or act on behalf of Datum.

---

## 5. Roles and Authority

Roles exist to assign responsibility and communicate areas of responsibility.

They do not create a general hierarchy of technical competence.

### 5.1 Cofounders

The Cofounders are responsible for:

- maintaining Datum's continuity;
- maintaining its governance;
- setting and protecting its overall direction;
- approving major external commitments;
- maintaining institutional records; and
- resolving matters that cannot otherwise be settled.

A Cofounder does not automatically possess technical authority over every project or discipline.

### 5.2 Project Leads

A project may designate a Project Lead.

The Project Lead is responsible for:

- maintaining clarity of the project objective;
- coordinating contributors;
- identifying blockers;
- keeping the project moving;
- maintaining appropriate records; and
- ensuring that the project reaches a meaningful conclusion.

Project leadership is responsibility, not authority over Datum as a whole.

### 5.3 Technical Authority

Technical decisions should generally be made by the people with the relevant expertise.

A title does not override evidence.

A founder can be wrong.

A new member can be right.

Technical disagreements should therefore be resolved through reasoning, evidence, experimentation, and review rather than status.

---

## 6. Decision Making

Datum should prefer discussion and technical consensus before formal voting.

Ordinary operational decisions may be decided by a simple majority of active members with voting rights.

The following require unanimous approval of the Cofounders:

- admission of a member;
- removal of a member;
- amendment of this Agreement;
- changes to the governance structure;
- changes to Cofounder rights or responsibilities;
- significant external commitments made in Datum's name;
- significant financial commitments;
- sale or exclusive licensing of significant Datum work;
- and termination of Epoch 2.

No individual member may create a significant financial, legal, or external obligation on behalf of Datum without the required approval.

Where new evidence materially changes a previous decision, the decision may be reconsidered.

---

## 7. Participation

Datum is not an employment organisation during Epoch 2.

Members are not expected to contribute identical amounts of time.

Different members may have different academic obligations, projects, responsibilities, and areas of expertise.

Members are expected to:

- contribute meaningfully to responsibilities they accept;
- communicate when they cannot fulfil those responsibilities;
- maintain sufficient records of important work; and
- avoid leaving active work dependent on undocumented personal knowledge.

Persistent non-participation may result in a review of a member's role or continued membership.

---

## 8. Projects

Datum projects may originate from:

- an individual member;
- several collaborating members; or
- the collective itself.

A project should have:

- a defined objective;
- people responsible for the work;
- an appropriate technical record;
- a meaningful output or research objective; and
- sufficient documentation for review or continuation.

A project may change direction when new evidence, constraints, or technical discoveries justify doing so.

Significant changes should be recorded.

A project does not need to become a commercial product to constitute meaningful Datum work.

---

## 9. Individual and Datum Work

A member's independent work does not automatically become Datum work merely because its creator is a Datum member.

Work may become a Datum project when:

- multiple Datum members materially collaborate;
- Datum resources are materially used;
- the work is explicitly undertaken under the Datum name;
- the work is initiated as a Datum project; or
- the work becomes materially connected to Datum's collective purpose.

Where relevant, project records should make the relationship between the individual contributor and Datum clear.

---

## 10. Intellectual Property and Attribution

Participation in Datum does not by itself transfer ownership of a member's intellectual property to Datum.

Pre-existing work remains with its original owner.

Third-party and open-source work remains subject to its applicable licence.

Where work is produced collaboratively, contributors should receive appropriate attribution.

Datum project status does not mean that individual contribution should be erased.

Project records should make reasonable efforts to identify significant contributors and their contributions.

Where a project requires formal ownership, assignment, incorporation, commercialisation, exclusive licensing, or another legal arrangement, that matter must be addressed separately in writing.

No such transfer should be assumed from membership or project participation alone.

---

## 11. Open Work

Datum's technical work should be open by default.

Work produced under Datum should generally be made publicly available through appropriate repositories, publications, documentation, or other technical records.

The purpose of open work is to allow others to:

- inspect it;
- learn from it;
- reproduce it;
- contribute to it;
- build upon it; and
- evaluate it.

Exceptions may be made where necessary because of:

- third-party intellectual property;
- licensing restrictions;
- privacy;
- safety;
- security;
- or another legitimate restriction.

Such restrictions should be deliberate rather than assumed.

---

## 12. Technical Standard

Datum values:

- technical competence;
- first-principles understanding;
- evidence;
- experimentation;
- intellectual honesty;
- documentation;
- and completion.

It is acceptable not to know something.

It is not acceptable to knowingly present an unsupported assumption as established technical understanding.

Members are encouraged to question conventional solutions and challenge assumptions when there is reason to do so.

Existing tools, libraries, frameworks, and established techniques may be used where appropriate.

First-principles thinking does not require members to reinvent every underlying system.

It requires sufficient understanding of the foundations, assumptions, mechanisms, and constraints of the systems being used.

---

## 13. Failure and Shipping

Failure during development is an expected part of serious engineering.

A failed experiment may be valuable.

A disproven hypothesis may be valuable.

A prototype that exposes a fundamental design flaw may be valuable.

Failure becomes problematic when work produces neither a meaningful result nor a meaningful lesson, particularly where this results from avoidable neglect, poor communication, or refusal to address known problems.

Datum considers a project shipped when it produces a meaningful external artefact or result.

Examples include:

- an open-source repository;
- a working prototype;
- a hardware system;
- a public demonstration;
- a deployed software system;
- a research paper;
- a technical report;
- an experiment and its results;
- a dataset;
- a design;
- or another meaningful technical output.

Shipping does not require commercial release.

It means the work exists outside the team's private conversations in a form that can be examined, used, reproduced, tested, or built upon.

---

## 14. Documentation and Records

Important technical knowledge should exist in durable records.

It should not exist only in:

- private messages;
- temporary files;
- Slack conversations;
- or individual memory.

Depending on the project, records may include:

- Git repositories;
- design documents;
- CAD;
- experiment logs;
- research notes;
- specifications;
- test results;
- architecture documents;
- issue discussions;
- or technical reports.

Documentation should be proportional to the work.

The purpose is continuity and technical clarity, not bureaucracy.

---

## 15. External Representation

Members may discuss and publish their own work and contributions.

Members must not make significant commitments on behalf of Datum without the required approval.

Members should distinguish between:

- personal opinions;
- personal projects;
- Datum projects;
- and official positions of Datum.

The use of the Datum name should accurately represent the relationship between the work and the collective.

---

## 16. External Work

A long-term ambition of Datum is to become a place where difficult technical problems can be brought to the collective.

During Epoch 2, however, Datum's primary focus is its own projects, research, capability, and credibility.

External requests should therefore be evaluated based on:

- technical value;
- member interest;
- available capacity;
- alignment with Datum's purpose;
- and impact on existing projects.

Datum should not accept external work merely to appear busy or important.

Formal external commitments require Cofounder approval.

---

## 17. Financial Matters

Participation in Datum does not create an entitlement to:

- salary;
- equity;
- profit;
- stipend;
- or reimbursement.

During Epoch 2, collective funds or resources may be used for legitimate Datum purposes, including:

- equipment;
- software;
- materials;
- fabrication;
- hosting;
- research resources;
- and necessary travel.

Significant expenditure requires appropriate approval.

During Epoch 2, money should primarily support Datum's technical work.

Commercial activity, revenue generation, sponsorship structures, paid external work, and other significant financial models should be evaluated as part of a future Epoch rather than assumed.

---

## 18. Governance Records

This Agreement and other significant governance documents are maintained under version control in the Datum governance repository.

Changes must be made in writing and committed to the repository.

Amendments to this Agreement require unanimous approval of the Cofounders.

The Git history forms part of Datum's historical record.

Historical Epoch documents should not be silently rewritten to reflect later circumstances.

---

## 19. Epoch Transition

Epoch 2 begins on 1 September 2026.

It continues until the Cofounders establish a subsequent Epoch or formally terminate Epoch 2.

At the end of an Epoch, Datum should review:

- completed work;
- unfinished work;
- failed projects;
- membership;
- responsibilities;
- technical capability;
- governance;
- and the direction of the collective.

A subsequent Epoch may revise membership, rules, project structures, and organisational scope based on the lessons of the previous Epoch.

A subsequent Epoch should establish its own governing framework rather than silently rewriting the historical record of Epoch 2.

---

## 20. Interpretation

This Agreement establishes the governing rules of Epoch 2.

It is intentionally not exhaustive.

Where a situation is not explicitly covered, Datum should favour:

1. the integrity of the collective;
2. evidence over hierarchy;
3. preservation of individual contribution and attribution;
4. the ability of members to do serious technical work;
5. openness where reasonably possible;
6. documentation of significant decisions;
7. minimal necessary bureaucracy; and
8. meaningful technical output.

The Agreement should be interpreted as a framework for enabling serious work, not as a substitute for judgement.

---

## 21. Acknowledgement

Participation in Datum Collective during Epoch 2 constitutes acknowledgement of the responsibilities and governance principles established by this Agreement.

Where a separate written project or appointment document exists, it should be read together with this Agreement.

A separate document may define matters specific to that project or responsibility but may not override the governing authority established by this Agreement unless explicitly approved as an amendment.
